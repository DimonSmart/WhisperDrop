# IDD-0008.adr-whisper-decoding-fallback

## Status

Accepted — 2026-10-25

## Context

WhisperDrop (Whisper.net 1.9.1, whisper.cpp 1.8.5) exhibited catastrophic repetitive decoding on a long, predominantly Spanish recording with intermittent English. The purpose of six-profile diagnostic runs was to select one understandable, maintainable recognition strategy rather than ship multiple configurable decoding modes.

The same recording was tested without VAD and with Silero VAD:

| Approach | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| Default greedy decoding, no VAD | After otherwise plausible text, repeated `[HABLANDO EN INGLÉS]` roughly 1,200 times; substantial speech lost. About 185 seconds. |
| Strict fallback, no VAD (`entropy_thold=1.8`, `logprob_thold=-0.8`) | Most useful complete transcript, without catastrophic looping. About 236 seconds. Some English passages represented by markers or minor recognition errors. |
| Beam Search, no VAD | Substantial, largely complete transcript with fewer missing English passages, but possible language/translation errors and around 502 seconds. |
| Initial temperature 0.2, no VAD | Another substantial repetition loop, around 748 seconds. |
| No-speech threshold 0.4, no VAD | Did not materially improve the strict-fallback result. |
| VAD enabled | Prevented the earlier catastrophic loop but introduced many short repeated phrases; each run took about 31–38 minutes. Current VAD integration restarted recognition separately per speech region. |

These are qualitative comparisons of transcripts from the same audio, **not** word-error-rate benchmarks against a verified reference transcript. The failure-free result on this recording is not a guarantee against all omissions or hallucinations.

The first production run with the strict primary pass and full-file Beam fallback still showed a repeated phrase (e.g. `es una tirolina` 14 times). Whole-file retry allowed the native greedy decoder to waste time repeating speech before any correction could start, and a replacement transcript could omit entire sections even if its repetition count improved. This motivated **early local repair**, superseding the initial whole-file fallback decision below.

## Decision

- Maintain one user-facing automatic recognition flow; do not reintroduce VAD or six diagnostic modes.
- Keep hard-coded primary thresholds `entropy_thold=1.8` and `logprob_thold=-0.8`. Preserve user choices (model, language, task, prompt, threads, device).
- Decode the 16 kHz mono prepared WAV into samples once, retaining the loaded Whisper model. Process the remaining audio starting from its current sample offset rather than restarting the full file.
- Observe **native segment callbacks**, maintaining a rolling tail of text; a contiguous 3–32-word group repeated at least 3 times, or two adjacent non-speech markers, triggers immediate cancellation of the active greedy run. Keep a three-segment lookbehind uncommitted. On cancellation, asynchronously await native shutdown before constructing a new processor, preserving already accepted segments and current partial preview.
- Rerun at most **25 seconds** from the last confirmed segment boundary using Beam Search. Evaluate each candidate using meaningful words, repeated phrases, non-speech markers such as `[MÚSICA]` and `[SPEAKING SPANISH]`, and actual segment timestamps; **never interpret an empty or marker-only candidate as proof that speech was absent**. For sparse or marker-dominated output, decode the *same* window again as two independent ~12.5-second greedy passes, retaining the more plausible result.
- Only commit the chosen full-window result when it passes the conservative content check. If both candidates remain suspicious, commit at most meaningful segments from the first half of the short-pass candidate and resume **from that half-window boundary**, not from the end of the entire repair window. Surface a warning and log the exact interval that may still be missing. Never append 25 seconds of text but restart 12.5 seconds into that same text (overlap would duplicate speech). An absent/very short final native segment timestamp must not justify skipping the full region.
- Limit local repairs to five; if exceeded, switch the remaining suffix to Beam once rather than permitting an unbounded greedy loop. If Beam fails for a bounded region, retry that region once with ordinary decoding and show a quality warning. Report repeated words still present in the bounded Beam result; do not silently delete transcription.
- Expose diagnostic lines prefixed `[WhisperDrop]` to stderr/terminal and write the **latest transcription** to `recognition-debug.log` in WhisperDrop's platform-local application-data folder (alongside `settings.json`). Include detection/resume time offsets, decoder/candidate, segment count, first and last segment timestamps, text length, meaningful word count, markers, repeat count, RMS level, selection rationale, unresolved intervals and elapsed time. Log only technical counters and timing diagnostics; do not write recognized speech excerpts. Diagnostic-file write errors must not prevent transcription.
- **Diagnostics-only addition (2026-10-26):** Record the accepted transcript's unusually long timestamped segments (≥15 seconds), large timestamp gaps between successive segments (≥5 seconds), significant overlaps (≥3 seconds), and positions outside the audio duration. Emit a full-transcript timestamp coverage summary even when no repetition recovery was triggered. For *every* local Beam or two-slice candidate, log the time range, union of clamped timestamp coverage, largest internal gap, leading/trailing uncovered intervals and summed out-of-window segment durations; flag suspicious candidate timestamp coverage as `TIMING-ANOMALY`. This specifically distinguishes a 25-second window with a 3-second result from a candidate whose last native timestamp is 26.96 seconds. These are diagnostic hints only: natural silence and Whisper timestamp errors are possible. **Do not use the new timing metrics to reject segments, change retry decisions, or suppress text.** Logging remains in `recognition-debug.log` alongside settings.
- **Missing-speech recovery experiment (2026-10-26):** After the regular recognition and its repetition repairs finish, inspect up to eight suspicious audio regions per file: internal timestamp gaps of 5–35 seconds and long (15–35-second) segments with fewer than 0.8 meaningful words per second. The main decoder, early loop cancellation and partial preview are unchanged. Gate probes by cheap 200 ms frame RMS energy measurements; *sound is not speech*, and this is not Silero or VAD segmentation.
- Independently transcribe a short candidate region using strict greedy, Beam Search and split-into-two strict greedy passes, without touching the primary text during evaluation. Log candidate word rates, non-speech marker counts, repetitions, coverage, elapsed time and whether the acoustic gate passed, without transcript excerpts. Use normalized token-overlap agreement between at least two *plausible* decoders (Dice ≥ 0.58 for gaps, ≥ 0.65 for sparse existing segments); consider length only among agreeing candidates. Reject candidates that mostly repeat adjacent recognized text. For replacement of a sparse existing segment, also require ≥60% more meaningful words and at least 12 additional words.
- Insert agreed speech into a true timeline gap or replace an unusually sparse existing segment in its original timestamp range. Strictly reject out-of-range candidate segment timestamps (except insignificant overhang), and normalize retained timestamps to the original bounded interval. Preserve unchanged text if there is silence, decoder disagreement, insufficient evidence or a processing error, and mark unresolved high-energy probes with a quality warning. Rebuild final transcript chronologically; do not append out-of-order gap candidates to the streaming preview. The new recovery is intentionally conservative and can miss real speech; agreement is a heuristic, not a ground-truth accuracy guarantee. This phase adds computation only for suspicious regions.
- Preserve the raw Whisper transcript for optional AI correction. Detecting completely missing speech is outside the scope of this simple repetition guard.

## Alternatives Considered

- Always use Beam Search: improved some multilingual sections, but substantially slower without a clear overall advantage.
- Increase initial temperature or tighten no-speech threshold: did not resolve looping in the observed runs.
- Use VAD for every file: substantially slower and currently introduces short repeated phrases and context degradation at region boundaries.
- Full-file post-hoc Beam retry: rejected after the observed production run because it starts too late, is slow, may lose material and replaces good text outside the error region.
- Persistent VAD segmentation, multi-decode voting, alignment and complex overlapping-stitch heuristics: rejected as disproportionate to the immediate requirement.

## Maintenance
- The runtime components are separated by responsibility: `WhisperRecognitionService.cs` (orchestration and recovery), `WhisperRepetitionGuard.cs` (loop heuristic), `WhisperRecognitionDiagnostics.cs` (logger and timing diagnostics), and `WhisperGapRecovery.cs` (bounded gap scoring). No old VAD or whole-file diagnostic decoding mode is shipped.
- The automatic `recognition-debug.log` remains available for operational troubleshooting, but removes raw transcript excerpts and duplicated experimental messages. Thresholds, local recovery choices, and segment insertion behavior are unchanged.

## Consequences

- Ordinary transcripts are decoded with the strict settings only once; repeated segments trigger local recovery while the original decode is still in progress.
- The repair boundary is a native Whisper segment end; it is not a forced arbitrary splice in the middle of a segment. The three pending segments are withheld until safe, so the live preview cannot show removed repeated text in ordinary circumstances.
- A short repair window resets native context and can still cause minor boundary inaccuracies. A single native segment callback may not fire until a large span is decoded, so interruption is immediate **at callback granularity**, not at every token.
- Automatic restart is restricted to a bounded local window, with a two-piece re-decode only when quality checks fail. The suspicious interval may still be incompletely recognized: warn and record it instead of claiming a successful 25-second repair. A pathological recording can fall back to one Beam suffix after five repairs.
- Both decoding thresholds are implementation constants, not saved user settings.
- The heuristic cannot prove accuracy, detect nonrepeating hallucinations, or guarantee recovery of omitted audio. Continue surfacing errors as quality warnings rather than fabricating or silently deleting speech.
